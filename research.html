MEDIA · CULTURE · NATIONAL IDENTITY

Popular Culture and National Identities

In an age where nations are increasingly experienced through screens rather than physical spaces, popular culture has become one of the most powerful arenas through which collective identities are imagined, negotiated and contested.

National identity has long been understood as an "imagined community", a concept coined by Benedict Anderson. It suggests that individuals who may never encounter one another can nevertheless feel connected through shared narratives of belonging (Cheah & Culler, 2003).

While pre-modern thinkers such as Thomas Hobbes connected identity to sovereign authority and political order, modernist theorists such as Ernest Gellner understood nationalism as a modern phenomenon associated with industrialisation, mass communication and culture (Kumar, 2017; Gellner, 2006). Gellner further emphasised the relationship between communication technologies and cultural homogeneity, demonstrating how media becomes an integral agent in shaping how nations are represented, remembered and consumed (Gellner, 1983).

In contemporary society, these narratives are neither static nor solely rooted in history or territory. Instead, they are continuously produced, mediated and reimagined through popular culture.

GENDER · TECHNOLOGY · DIGITAL JUSTICE

Pixels, Power, and Patriarchy: Rethinking Gendered Justice in the Digital Age

The urgency to rethink gendered justice in the digital age lies in the gap between technology companies' promises of progress and the lived experiences of people navigating digital spaces.

This research explores whose voices are amplified in digital environments, whether truly inclusive spaces can be created, and who is left behind in the data economy. It considers what justice means in an era where identity, agency, resistance and freedom are increasingly intertwined with technology.

Through discussions around intersectionality, the decolonisation of gender and justice narratives, and the importance of local experiences, the seminar seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical discussions and practical realities.

MEDIA REPRESENTATION · GEOPOLITICS · NEWS

Russia and Its Perception by Western and Russian State Media

Russia has often been projected by Western media as a defensive power, while simultaneously being portrayed as an aggressive and isolated state under Vladimir Putin (Götz & Merlen, 2019). Russia's political positioning and international image have consequently become subjects of competing media narratives.

These differences in representation can shape public perceptions of Russia, particularly in a global media environment where Western narratives have considerable international influence. The conflict between these narratives raises questions about how audiences understand Russia and its role in international affairs.

This research examines how Russia is represented by Western news organisations, particularly BBC and CNN, in comparison with Russian state media outlet RT. It further explores how these contrasting representations contribute to the formation of public opinion about Russia.

ADVERTISING · CONSUMER BEHAVIOUR · IDENTITY

To What Extent Does the Depiction of Black Pete in Advertisements Affect Consumers' Purchase Intentions Across Different Demographic Groups?

This study focuses on purchase intention, which refers to consumers' willingness to buy a particular product and can also serve as a strategic forecasting tool for brands (Maslikhan, 2019).

Previous research has identified a positive relationship between advertising and consumers' purchase intentions (Mirabi et al., 2015). Building on this understanding, the study investigates how different representations of Black Pete in advertisements influence consumer behaviour.

By examining advertisements featuring both traditional and modern depictions of Pete, the research seeks to understand whether consumer purchase intentions differ across demographic groups. These include political attitudes, race, generation and nationality.

**Research question:** To what extent does the depiction of Black Pete in advertisements affect consumers' purchase intentions across different demographic groups?

05

DIGITAL CULTURE · GENDER · POLARISATION

To What Extent Are 'Alpha Male' Podcasts on Social Media Associated with Polarisation Between Men and Women?

The growing presence of 'alpha male' podcasts across social media platforms has raised questions about their potential influence on gender attitudes and social relationships.

This study focuses on content circulated through platforms such as TikTok and Instagram, investigating the extent to which 'alpha male' podcast content reinforces more extreme gender perspectives.

The research explores whether exposure to this content is associated with increasing polarisation between men and women, particularly in how they understand gender roles, relationships and one another.

**Research question:** To what extent do 'Alpha Male' podcasts on social media influence polarisation between men and women?
